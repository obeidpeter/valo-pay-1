# Audit remediation — 26 September 2026

This release addresses the twelve findings from the comprehensive audit of PR #60 / main commit `9ae9f8afec9e2e3f9070809c5026f0ff182d74ce`. The fixes preserve recorded decisions and financial evidence. Where old records cannot establish intent or ownership, they require an explicit review rather than inventing it.

## Existing data and rollout

Apply migration 009 before publishing this application build, in every application and isolated runtime schema. See [database migrations](database-migrations.md) and the [identity preflight](record-identity-migration.md). Duplicate references or deliveries stop the transaction without deleting evidence. Review and correct genuine historical duplicates through an approved recorded process before retrying.

Unversioned reversal decisions receive a separate renewed-review exception on reconciliation. Finance or an administrator must inspect the retained evidence and choose the appropriate current outcome. Do not remove the hold by editing the original exception or releasing its instalment as an ordinary dispute.

Review fix to FIN-02, made after the review of PRs #61 to #67: this release as first deployed put every instalment a renewed review held into dispute, paid ones and ones unpaid after their final attempt included, so releasing one returned it to collection and the next close gave up on it again with a new exception; it also held payments with the reversal's reference through other connections, and instalments through allocations no longer in use. A hold now reaches exactly what adopting the decision would reverse, and live allocations: the payments the reversal names through its own connection, or, when no payment of its own connection has its reference, the one payment of another connection an adopted decision reverses (the first made with its reference, when it agrees with the reversal), with the instalments their evidence names; the repair of the holds this release as first deployed wrote keeps that hold, and that instalment paused, while the review is open. It never changes a paid or finally unpaid instalment's status; one still being collected is paused in dispute with the status it had, and gets exactly that status back at the first reconciliation after the review is resolved, with no release, unless a dispute was recorded for it meanwhile. Instalments this release as first deployed put in dispute get the status their records establish at the next reconciliation (paid with nothing outstanding, unpaid after its final attempt once the retry engine gave up on it, otherwise the status of its balance and attempts), and releasing such a dispute returns that status too; one with a customer dispute recorded since its last release stays in dispute for the dispute workflow, as the records cannot show whether the hold or the dispute came first.

Previously combined settlement batches, whose evidence names more than one provider connection or whose identity another batch shares, stay in variance. Their earlier totals, provider identities and statement links are preserved. Run reconciliation for every existing lender after rollout before presenting refreshed settlement results; historical snapshots remain historical. This release deliberately provides no generic edit or automatic release for that ambiguous historical state: ordinary reimport and reconciliation do not clear the quarantine. Once the providers have confirmed whose payout a held batch is, Finance or an administrator releases it by resolving its exception as provider identity confirmed, the only resolution its exception accepts (see the review fix below). Where the providers cannot attribute the payout to one connection, the batch stays held and its exception stays open while a data owner establishes the real provider-scoped breakdown and commissions a reviewed data repair; once the repaired evidence names one connection, the next reconciliation releases the batch and closes the exception as its condition cleared. Every held batch gets an exception of its own for the hold, including one held while another variance was open for it, and a batch whose hold's exception was closed another way before this review fix gets a renewed review at the first reconciliation after rollout, for Finance or an administrator to confirm whose payout it is. Fresh provider-scoped records no longer merge solely because their batch references coincide.

Review fix to FIN-03, made after the review of PRs #61 to #67: this release as first deployed held every earlier batch whose connection, which earlier builds copied to it from its first payment, differed from its evidence, so a legitimate single-connection batch (such as a settlement file naming "Sandbox Rail Settlements" for debits seen through "Sandbox Rail", with an unlabelled statement credit) was frozen for good. A batch is now held only when its linked evidence names more than one provider connection, or another batch claims its identity; evidence that names no provider or connection carries no identity and never makes a batch ambiguous, and an earlier batch takes the connection its own evidence names. A batch it held although the batch is not ambiguous is released at the next reconciliation, once: the release is recorded beside the kept snapshot, the lines held meanwhile, never counted, are read as new lines are, the hold's exception closes and the audit entry names the batch. A batch Finance entered by hand claims the provider it records only when it records one. Genuinely ambiguous batches stay held until Finance or an administrator confirms whose payout the batch is, as the finding asked for a way out that this release as first deployed left to a data repair outside the application. The hold's exception is resolved as provider identity confirmed, naming one of the connections the batch was held for: only Admin or Finance may record it, and only a recorded identity is accepted. The next reconciliation releases the batch, once, as that connection's payout. Its evidence of that connection, and evidence that names none, stays with it; each settlement line of another connection is counted in that connection's own batch and leaves the batch's gross, fee and net, unless they were typed by hand, and always its expected fee, which Finance cannot type; and each statement credit of another connection is left to link to its own connection's batch. The release records the identity, who confirmed it and when, and what moved, the audit entry names them, and the resolution cannot change afterwards. The hold's exception accepts no other resolution while the batch is held: the settlement codes this release as first deployed offered for it closed the exception while the batch stayed held with its evidence uncounted, with nothing left to release it. It concerns only the hold: every held batch has one, created beside any other variance open for the batch, and no other report is carried on it. One this release as first deployed made carry a report of a collection counted in two batches stays open for that report once the batch is released, and then offers the settlement codes; a confirmation settles no report, which is raised again on its own. Where the providers cannot attribute the payout to one connection, the exception stays open until the data owner repairs the evidence, and the next reconciliation then releases the batch and closes the exception as its condition cleared. A batch still held whose hold's exception was resolved another way, or closed in any way without a confirmed identity, gets a renewed review at the next reconciliation, once: its notes say that the earlier resolution keeps its meaning, but that the batch stays held until Finance or an administrator confirms whose payout it is, which is the review's only resolution, and the release follows a confirmation on either exception. A later hold of a released batch, which only an edit outside reconciliation can cause since the record API and imports no longer accept the links reconciliation records, keeps the release in the batch's history, is not decided by the earlier confirmation, and gets an exception of its own.

The retired-cookie change affects anonymous browsers that still hold only an old cookie: they start a new sample workspace. Existing current cookies and staff accounts retain their identity. No database records are deleted by this change. The old browser cookie is deliberately not accepted as proof of ownership for restoration.

## Host checks

The Clerk proxy permits 240 requests per minute per client network (IPv6 /64), 8 concurrent requests per network and 32 per process. It bounds each attempt to 30 seconds, requests to 16 MiB, unknown-length buffered replies to 4 MiB and known-length replies to 32 MiB. Quota/concurrency refusals are 429 with `Retry-After: 60`; oversized requests return 413; unsupported `Expect` handshakes return 417; upstream failures return 502/504 or terminate a reply whose headers were already sent. Disconnecting cancels upstream work. Limits reset on process restart and multiply across instances; the edge remains responsible for fleet-wide quotas and trusted forwarding headers.

Before commissioning a host, verify sign-in with its configured Clerk instance, readiness after migration, real provider test credentials when available, private export storage, monitoring delivery and backup recovery. Offline and disposable-database tests do not establish that those external services have been configured. No production database change, real payment, or live credential test is performed by this release's local verification.
