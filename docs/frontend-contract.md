# Valo Pay console contract

The beginner usability programme, deployed with PR #79, adds public `/help` with searchable task guides and a glossary; reading it does not initialise a workspace or grant access. Contextual links select a task and allow only static internal return destinations; help opened from a Cash Desk section (`?view=accounting`, `vat` or `payroll`) returns to that section. Overview offers role-appropriate next actions and an optional summary derived from the existing pilot records. Reading-guide progress is distinct from committed imports, matches and approved closes. Find a page searches static navigation labels and descriptions, not customer records; its count is in a live region present before the first search, and Clear page search returns focus to the search box. Cash Desk places source clocks and coverage beside balance totals, and distinguishes missing evidence from a genuine zero. See the [programme register](usability-2026-09-29/README.md) and [validation](usability-2026-09-29/validation.md) for its evidence and limits.

The pilot operations release adds `/sources` (versioned mappings, delivery checks and Paystack fixtures), `/work` (personal assignments and supervisor workload), `/close-review` (independent Finance decisions and exact-snapshot exports), and `/lifecycle` (administrator retention policies, holds, deletion previews and receipts). A retention policy keeps the workspace's minimum periods (`minimumDays` in the view: 30 days in the sandbox; in a staff pilot six years for raw CSV and export files and 366 days for request payloads), and in a staff pilot a preview's preparer cannot approve it (`secondApprover`; runs name `preparedBy`, and the console disables that administrator's approval, while the sandbox only explains the rule). `/team` also shows explicit lender grants and host commissioning status. See [pilot operations controls](pilot-operations-controls.md) for the persistence, authorisation and external-configuration boundaries.

All data is a persistent, isolated **synthetic sandbox**. No payment or SMS is actually sent. No live data upload is permitted. Use British spelling, NGN (kobo / 100) and Africa/Lagos dates. Always say “We never hold money”. Keep a visible “Sandbox · synthetic data” label and current Observation mode. No claims of compliance, live success, or gate passes from seed data.

## Workflow guidance and task queues

`/presentation` is an optional investor demonstration preparation page inside the console. It offers six talking points, a downloadable presenter brief, synthetic CSV files and self-reported preparation checks. The guide persists only in this browser session, scoped to the viewer and lender; invalid saved state is ignored. Starting, advancing, ending or clearing preparation checks never submits operational actions. Presenter notes are collapsed and explicitly labelled as visible on the shared screen. The match talking point opens the sample customer’s history (`/customers/:id`, found by the reference PRES-C001, or `/customers?q=PRES-C001` until the pack is imported), where the payment’s automatic, certain R1 match is shown with its explanation; it never appears in Matches to review. The guide renders above the page’s error boundary, so a page error keeps the toolbar and End presentation. Existing sandbox labels, permissions, source gaps and pending reviews remain visible. See [presentation preparation](investor-presentation.md).

The [evidence-led usability release](usability/README.md) keeps navigation stable while showing the actual demo role, lender operating mode and WAT separately from environment. Queue scroll offsets are held only in memory for the current lender/actor/role, with a bounded map; returning to a visited filtered route restores its position. Paging, filtering or searching on the same page changes only the query string and leaves the scroll position where it is (`lib/queue-position.ts`). This is separate from the existing saved-view preferences.

Forms distinguish a structured initial rejection from an unknown result after a lost response. Unknown writes retain the original payload and idempotency key, hold changed inputs and offer an exact retry. Later authentication/policy refusal does not turn that original outcome into a known failure, unless the refusal says `operation: "cancelled"`: the service has closed that request's journal entry, so neither the original nor any retry with its key was or can be saved, and the console releases the held request and shows the refusal. Every notice about an unconfirmed request also offers a way out after a warning that the original may have been saved: Discard original request, which points to Operations, or closing the record and review dialogs. Every notice about a change the operations journal records, and the record and review dialogs' close confirmations, say that a request the service received stays in Operations after the form is closed or the page reloaded, and the notices link there (Open Operations, `OpenOperations` in `components/pilot-ui.tsx`); the demo role switch, which the journal does not record, has no such link. The pages whose changes the journal does not record (Team & access, its staff pilot set-up checks and the pilot journey's lender set-up) ask before they are left or reloaded while a change is being sent or its outcome is unconfirmed (`useUnsavedChanges`), since the page is then the only way to check it. A new submission then gets a new key. A definitive refusal (400, 403, 404, 409, 410, 413, 415, 422) ends its key, so an identical resubmission is a new request; a 401 or 429 keeps it, in forms and connected actions alike: such a refusal is not final, and any journal entry the request left stays pending until the same key is sent again. An answer that names the key's journal entry as `completed`, `running` or `pending` also keeps the key (`requestOpen` in `lib/safe-mutations.ts`): a request with it was saved, is still running or is not confirmed, so a failure is held as unconfirmed and retried with the same key, and a refusal does not end the key. The connected pages end a held action on a refusal (a 4xx) that names its entry as `completed` (`savedAnswerWithheld`): the action was saved earlier and the service withholds its saved answer under the current permissions or review, so no retry can recover it. They show the service's words, release the action and load the workspace again. Recovery state is not persisted in the browser through reload or deliberate abandonment, and a reloaded form does not look up its own request: recovery after a reload is manual, from Operations, whose link in the navigation shows how many of the person's requests in the lender wait for confirmation. Check-only import failures remain editable; an import check never commits. Consequential dialogs start at their decision evidence, and correction links focus associated fields.

Allocation confirmation and rejection (`confirm_allocation`, `reject_allocation`) require `proposalId` and `proposalUpdatedAt` in the action data: the proposed allocation the decision was made on and its `updatedAt` as it was read (the contract's `AllocationDecisionData`). The console sends both. A request without either is refused (400, naming `data.proposalId` or `data.proposalUpdatedAt`) once the lender is loaded, before the payment or its proposal is read: nothing is saved, and a keyed request's journal entry is cancelled. Like any write, it first waits for another write to the same lender, and is answered 503 if that lender stays busy. The server checks the pair against the payment's current proposed allocation before changing anything: another proposal, or one changed since it was read, is 409. `proposalUpdatedAt` is an RFC 3339 date and time compared as an instant, as a record's `expectedUpdatedAt` is, so the same instant written with an offset names the same version. **Breaking change for API clients:** the pair used to be optional, and a request without it acted on whichever proposal was current when it arrived; such a request is now refused. At allocation commit, the server also checks current payment credit, current instalment balance and cancelled/closed/disputed state, and refuses an already-applied allocation. An audit correction displays its restored-balance effect and does not imply a refund.

The landing page offers a user-started preview of the actual console and a pilot enquiry email link. Loading the landing page alone still creates no workspace. Selecting Load interactive preview creates or resumes the same sample workspace used by the full console; the caption explains that its changes persist there. The preview omits the personal guide with `embedded=1`.

The console guide has five optional steps: inspect a customer, review a match, follow up an overdue exception, run daily close and export a dispute pack. Progress is stored per lender in the browser and is explicitly self-reported, not operational evidence. It can be dismissed, reopened and restarted.

Overview queues link to the relevant view: `/mandates?view=awaiting-activation`, `/reconciliation?view=review`, `/reconciliation?view=duplicates`, `/collections?view=failed` and `/exceptions?view=overdue`. URL parameters preserve task filters through navigation; exceptions also support due-today, owner and type filters. Urgent records sort first. A deadline written as a day alone (an exception's `dueBy`, a mandate's `activationDeadline`, an instalment's `dueDate`) lasts that whole West Africa Time day: it is due today until 23:59:59.999 WAT and overdue only after it, and a date-only deadline sorts by the end of its day. The queues, their counts, the overview's overdue count, the alerts, the daily close and the console's own overdue labels read it the same way (`deadlineEnds` and `deadlinePassed` in `lib/valopay-schema`); a deadline that is not a real date is no deadline. Collection rows show amount, outstanding amount, due date, owner and next action; CSV import remains available in a secondary disclosure.

Collection next-action links identify the relevant mandate, customer event or instalment for payment review. The destination identifies and focuses that record, and Back to collections restores the view, owner filter and originating row, including its page. Collection next-action return addresses are restricted to the collections route and the same lender. Customer links can also return to Customers, Mandates, Exceptions or Reconciliation; these addresses are validated against the same internal-route and lender boundary. An unavailable record or a link belonging to another lender is explained instead of silently opening unrelated work. A deep link to a record or a section, such as Review matches on Reports (`/reconciliation#precision-audit`), focuses and scrolls to its target once, when it arrives: paging or filtering on that page afterwards changes only the query string, so the view and focus stay with the table being paged.

Customers and Audit log request one page from the API using `limit` and `offset`, with 25, 50 or 100 rows and a filtered total. Search waits for a 300 ms pause before requesting results, and lender or search changes reset the page. Exceptions, Mandates and Collections request server-filtered priority pages of 25, 50 or 100 rows. Search matches customer names and record references without case or accent sensitivity; a record list's search also reads the text and number values in a record's data, never a field's name or JSON's own syntax. Page, size and filters are lender-bound URL state, so browser Back and customer return links restore the queue. A page number beyond the last page is corrected in place, replacing the address instead of adding a history entry, so Back leaves the list rather than returning to the out-of-range page. Saved views store a queue's filters (view, owner and type) per person, lender and queue in this browser, never its search text or result rows, and opening one clears the current search. Views an earlier build saved with their search, under `valopay-queue-views-v1:` keys, are removed from the browser when a queue with saved views is opened. Reconciliation requests six separate server-paged queues with complete totals and only the related records needed by each page. Each table keeps its current rows until its next page arrives, its frame is not a scroll anchor, and the pager (`components/record-pagination.tsx`) holds its own place on the screen until the page has loaded, since WebKit does not anchor the view, so the table, its pager and the pressed button stay where they are, including on a shorter last page. Every other paged list, the pickers and the pilot pages' lists included, keeps its rows in the same way (`keepRowsWhilePaging` in `lib/use-record-pagination.ts`). The allocation picker fetches a searchable page of instalments when opened; its search waits for a 300 ms pause before requesting results, and typing returns the picker to its first page by replacing the address, so Back leaves the page rather than stepping through letters. Precision review pages the seeded previous-month sample, including superseded allocations already reviewed as wrong. Reports request compact, server-filtered daily-close summaries; opening a close fetches its full evidence. Inclusive WAT date comparisons use the first and latest closes across the entire selected range, independent of the visible page. Current operational and billing measures remain unchanged. Customer timeline sections still page their rendered rows. Printing a paginated list includes only the visible page, with its range and total and an explicit notice when other pages are omitted.

Creating a mandate, the customer picker asks for one page of customers at a time, searched by name or reference (after a 300 ms pause, as the allocation picker's search waits), never the whole book; a chosen customer stays in the list while the person searches or pages on. Mandate confirmations show the customer, mandate reference, debit limit, current and resulting state, consequences and available recovery. Resuming a suspended mandate does not restore cancelled attempts. Cancellation cannot be undone; reissue creates a separate mandate with fresh consent evidence, leaves existing instalments linked as before and provides a link to the new record. Fresh-consent fields are not prefilled with the previous evidence. Policy and template reviews show version context, available previous values and synthetic sample messages; a missing or ambiguous predecessor is identified. Approval remains separate from sending a message or applying a new policy to existing mandates.

Money forms accept naira with at most two decimal places and submit exact integer kobo. Manual allocation previews remaining payment credit and instalment balance. Proposed-match decisions show both records and the allocation impact before submission; confirm and reject submit the payment ID required by the action contract. Reconciliation refreshes related records and dashboard reports after a change.

Evidence retains a searchable register across all gates, with owner, evidence date, status and edit controls. Reports distinguish no reviewed matches from a measured zero and identify the reporting period. A failed read is repeated automatically, at most twice, only when no answer arrived or the service failed (a 5xx or 408); a refusal such as 401, 403, 404, 409 or 429 shows at once. Query failures offer retry; actions and exports show useful success or failure feedback. Staff reviews confirm four named tasks individually. Settings label the prepared Paystack adapter as not connected: external verification and direct-debit capability confirmation remain necessary.

Customer history and the case page distinguish a confirmed 404 (Customer not found, Case not found) from a temporary loading failure; temporary failures retain a retry control. Audit verification records the lender and check time, clears its result when a new check starts or the lender changes, and discards late responses from an earlier visit. A successful check describes the entries checked at that time, not future entries or another lender's log.

### Effective daily-close status

Overview and Settings expose `closeSchedule`; Reports carries it in `operational.closeSchedule`. It combines the lender preference with actual process status: `enabled`, `automatic`, `runtimeState`, `serviceIssue`, `time`, `nextAt`, `missed`, `overdueMinutes`, `lateAfterMinutes`, `lastAt`, `lastTrigger`, `lastCheckedAt` and `lastErrorAt`, and the optional `failedAttempts`, `retryAt` and `pausedForInactivityAt`. A requested schedule is automatic only when this process is running the scheduler and has a recent successful service check. `nextAt` is null otherwise, and the legacy Overview `nextClose` is empty. `failedAttempts` counts failed automatic attempts at the pending time and `retryAt` is when the next is due, null unless automatic; `pausedForInactivityAt` says when the scheduler switched off the automatic close of a sandbox nobody changed for seven days. Answers saved by earlier builds, such as a replayed settings change, lack the three optional fields. `runtimeState` is `external` on a host whose closes run from a separate scheduled job (`VALOPAY_CLOSE_SCHEDULER=external`): this process runs none, so nothing is automatic and `nextAt` is null, but `missed` is reported as for a running service, with the overview's `close_missed` alert, where `off` reports none. The interface distinguishes intentionally off, run from a scheduled job, paused for inactivity, unavailable, starting, failed, delayed, retrying, due and missed states and offers manual closing where appropriate. Saving the lender preference does not start the service.

The service check time is a process heartbeat, not proof that this lender's close completed. Completed-close history comes from this lender's records. Heartbeat age uses the API host clock through the internal `observedAt` value; due times and completed-close records use the database clock. A heartbeat is delayed after the greater of two minutes or three scheduler intervals. Overview, Reports and Settings refresh their queries every 60 seconds while active. A failed refresh on these pages, in the Reports close history or on a connected page keeps the figures already shown, with a small notice (`RefreshProblem` in `components/load-problem.tsx`) that they could not be refreshed, when they were last updated and Try again; the full problem card is left for a first load with nothing to show. These observations do not establish an availability guarantee or enable live operations.

## Records
API `ValopayRecord`: id, merchantId, kind, name, status, reference, amountKobo, customerId, createdAt, updatedAt, data (metadata). amountKobo is in kobo, except where a kind with a currency field (a payment, payment evidence, or an exception about money in another currency) names another currency in data.currency: it then holds that currency's minor units (cents for USD, yen for JPY), and a reader shows it in that currency, never as naira. A data.currency on any other kind, such as a currency column an import kept as detail on an instalment, a mandate or an attempt, is not the amount's currency: that amount is in kobo, and the console shows it as naira (before, the customer page showed such an instalment of ₦1,000,000.00 as JPY 100,000,000).

Every status list, enum, failure code, exception type and resolution code below is defined once in `lib/valopay-schema` (`@workspace/valopay-schema`) and consumed by the API validator and the console. Never retype a value from this document; import it.

Kinds and data fields:
- customers: name, reference, status active; data bankName, accountMasked, phoneMasked, consentProvenance.
Every kind's data fields, including the ones the platform sets, are declared with their types in `lib/valopay-schema/src/records.ts` (`recordDataSchemas`, one schema per kind; `RecordDataOf<K>` is the inferred type). The API reads and writes record data through those types (`TypedRecord<K>` from `recordsOf`, `findRecord` and `makeRecord`), so the schema is the source and the summary below follows it.

- mandates: customerId, amountKobo=limit, reference provider ref, status per the TRD 4.2 machine (draft/submitted/pending_activation/active/suspended/expired/cancelled/failed; `mandateTransitions`); data workflow (`activationWorkflows`), frequency (`mandateFrequencies`), activationDeadline, consentEvidence, consentGaps, consentGiven, policyId, origin (created/imported/reissued), reminderCount, reissuedFrom. A PATCH may only follow the machine and never into cancelled or suspended; use the actions. The limit is part of the customer's consent (MAN-02): a PATCH that changes amountKobo is refused (400), and `mandate_reissue` with new consent evidence carries a new limit. RET-07: data.consentPolicyId, consentPolicyVersion and consentPolicySummary are pinned by the validator from data.policyId when the mandate is created (and by mandate_reissue, which accepts an optional data.policyId of an approved version of the same policy); they are server-owned, a direct policyId change is refused, and policyVersionHistory records each move made by apply_policy_version.
- due-items: customerId, reference, amountKobo, status scheduled/in_collection/partially_paid/paid/unpaid_final/in_dispute/cancelled/closed (derived; never set by PATCH: an edit rebuilds outstandingKobo from the confirmed allocations and the status follows it, so an instalment with nothing outstanding is paid and its unsent attempts are cancelled, one with part outstanding is partially_paid, and one with its full balance keeps scheduled or in_collection; in_dispute, cancelled and closed are kept, and unpaid_final is kept while money is owed. Each reconciliation re-derives any status that contradicts the stored balance and reports the count as `dueStatusesRepaired`. An instalment leaves in_dispute only when its customer dispute is resolved as not upheld or Finance releases it with `release_dispute`: its status then follows its balance (paid, partially_paid, or scheduled or in_collection by its attempts; the collections queue reads overdue from the due date), and a disputed debit the release covered does not put it back in dispute; one a renewed reversal review's hold put in dispute instead gets back the status it had before the hold (see `resolve_exception`); data dueDate, mandateId, owner (`executionOwners`; the TRD spelling `valo` is accepted and stored as `valopay`), outstandingKobo, overrideReason (needed between ₦5,000 and the merchant minimum from settings), amendedAt (set when amount or dueDate change), experimentId/experimentArm/firstFailureAt (immutable once assigned), disputeRelease {via (not_upheld or finance_release), releasedAt, releasedBy, reason, exceptionId, attemptId (the last counted attempt when it was released), status, outstandingKobo} (the latest release from dispute; an edit cannot change it), legacyReversalReviewIds (the open renewed reversal reviews holding it) and legacyReversalReviewPause {status, pausedAt, inferred} (the status such a hold paused in dispute and gives back; inferred when its records established that status, for one an earlier build's hold put in dispute), which reconciliation records and an edit cannot change.
- attempts: customerId, amountKobo, status scheduled/sent/succeeded/failed/unknown/cancelled/reversed; data dueItemId, number (across every source), source=external, failureCode (normalised to the TRD 4.4 catalogue `failureCodes`; unmapped codes become UNKNOWN with rawFailureCode kept), occurredAt, providerReference (the debit reference the ladder's R1 matches on), noticeId.
- observations: customerId, reference canonical provider reference, amountKobo, status resolved/unresolved; data source (`observationSources`), dueItemId (a strong link the ladder treats as R1 when the amount matches), narration, paymentId, resolutionKey, batchReference, feeKobo, grossAmountKobo, occurredAt, reversed, provider=Sandbox Rail, providerConnection (the connection the evidence came through; provider when absent, and the lender's provider when neither is given), currency (NGN when absent). A grossAmountKobo, when given, is at least amountKobo: a gross below what was received is refused, with 400 for a record and as an invalid row in an import. Reconciliation records countedInBatchId on a settlement line whose collection another batch already counts, providerIdentityHeld on a settlement line linked to a batch held for its provider identity and not counted in it (removed when the batch is released and the line counted), otherCurrencyLine on a settlement line in another currency than its batch (settlementBatchId names that batch), duplicateStatementCredit on a statement credit that repeats one already counted, and otherCurrencyCredit on a statement credit in another currency than the batch it names (see settlement-batches). Reconciliation records countedGrossKobo, assumedFeeKobo and expectedFeeKobo on a settlement line its batch counts: the gross, the fee and the schedule's fee on that gross the line adds to the batch's totals, in the line's currency, with no expectedFeeKobo for a line in a currency with no fee schedule (see settlement-batches). A line's fee is always checked against one schedule, at its first count and at every recount: that of the provider it names (data.provider), else of the connection it names (data.providerConnection), else of the lender's provider. The line alone decides it, never its payment's connection, which is that of whichever evidence arrived first, so a line gets the same schedule whichever of it and its payment arrived first. A line keeps the expected fee it was counted with until a recount moves it (its payment's gross completing it, a line an earlier build counted before its gross, or a line read again when the batch that counts its collection changes), so a count saved under an earlier rule stays as it was: a line naming a provider and another connection that PR #61 to #64 counted against its connection's schedule, or a line naming no provider that an earlier build counted against its payment's connection's schedule. Creating payment evidence through the record API or an import refuses (400, or an invalid row) a payment (paymentId), a resolution (resolutionKey or a resolved status) and the links only reconciliation records: settlementBatchId, resolvedTo, countedInBatchId, duplicateSettlementLine and otherCurrencyLine; saved payment evidence cannot be edited. Evidence of a reversal that was held for Finance and resolved any way but as the same payment, or that waited for a payment no connection had seen and was resolved as platform state confirmed, is set aside at the next reconciliation: it is resolved with resolutionKey reversal_set_aside_after_review and resolvedTo `exception:<id>`, no payment is made from it, and it is applied to none, even one found later. Held evidence Finance resolved as not money is set aside the same way with resolutionKey set_aside_after_review, and is never merged into a payment; held evidence Finance resolved as the same payment is resolved to that payment with resolutionKey joined_after_review; and a waiting reversal Finance resolved as provider state adopted is resolved to its payment when that arrives, with resolutionKey adopted_after_review when the payment came through another spelling of the connection.
- payments: customerId, reference, amountKobo; status unallocated/proposed/allocated/partial/overpaid/possible_duplicate/returned; data channel, observedAt, and the four independent dimensions collectionStatus (received/succeeded/failed/unknown), settlementStatus (unsettled/settled/variance), reversalStatus (none/reversed), refundStatus (none/requested/refunded); dueItemId, allocatedKobo, explanation, rejectedDueItemIds (instalments Finance said the payment does not belong to; automatic matching never pairs them again), currency and providerConnection (the evidence's, see Payment evidence and the matching ladder under Mutations), payerIdentification {customerId, identifiedBy, identifiedAt, reason, dueItemId, allocationId} (Finance identified the payer of evidence that named none, see `manual_allocate`), payerIdentificationHistory [{customerId, identifiedBy, identifiedAt, reason, dueItemId, allocationId, withdrawnBy, withdrawnAt, withdrawnReason}] (identifications withdrawn with the match that made them, see `review_allocation`), duplicateReview {exceptionId, resolutionCode, reviewedBy, reviewedAt} (a suspected duplicate Finance resolved as distinct_payments; it is never held again for the same reason), evidenceConflict {paymentId, exceptionId, resolutionCode} (made from evidence that conflicted with the payment sharing its key, or came through another connection than the payment with its reference, once Finance resolved that exception), grossUnstated (made from a settlement line that stated only its net amount; the debit's own gross raises the amount), evidenceReference (a pay-by-bank receipt Finance confirmed after its outcome stayed unknown: the masked reference of the evidence that the money arrived), legacyReversalReviewIds (the open renewed reversal reviews holding it: it takes no allocation, confirmation or automatic match; see `resolve_exception`). A payment's payer never changes once recorded, except that a payer Finance identified is withdrawn, back to none, when a precision review or a rejection has taken the identifying allocation out of use and nothing of the payment stays applied (see `review_allocation`). Money waiting for Finance (`paymentAwaitsAllocation`) is what an unallocated, partial or overpaid payment holds unapplied; a payment with nothing unapplied, such as one an earlier build made for ₦0, waits for no one. Money in another currency than NGN is held for Finance and is never part of a customer's naira position or credit. Legacy spellings not_reversed/not_refunded are read as none. A payment whose money all went back to the payer (reversed, or refunded in full outside Valo Pay) is `returned`: its proposals are withdrawn, it cannot be allocated (409), it is in no allocation queue or ageing check, and its money is not customer credit (`paymentUnappliedKobo`, `paymentMoneyReturned`). A refund recorded after part of a payment was applied returns only the rest, such as an overpayment's excess: the applied money stays on its instalments and the payment is `allocated`. That money never went back, so if its allocation is later superseded the payment is `unallocated` again and holds it as customer credit for Finance to allocate; automatic matching, which compares the gross amount, leaves such a payment for Finance with the reason, and an allocation of more than it holds is refused (409). data.refundedKobo records what a refund returned: `record_refund` returns the money the payment had not applied, and the pay-by-bank refund returns the whole receipt after taking its allocations off the instalments. A refund recorded before the field existed is read as the whole payment, so that payment stays `returned` whatever happens to its allocations. Billing, the uplift report and the overview read the applied money that stands (`paymentAppliedKobo`): allocatedKobo less anything a refund took back, and nothing once reversed. A settlement line on a direct debit sets collectionStatus succeeded, since the debit was collected even when its webhook never arrived, and billing reads a settled direct debit stored as received the same way. A payment's status always follows its records: a payment with money applied is never `unallocated`, and each close re-derives any status that contradicts them (`paymentStatusesRepaired`); a payment the rule ladder cannot apply is left for Finance with the reason (`paymentsSkipped`) instead of failing the close.
- allocations: customerId, amountKobo, status confirmed/proposed/superseded; data paymentId, dueItemId, rule, confidence, explanation, reviewed (correct/wrong), automatic, confirmedAt (when it was applied; a precision review or a reinstatement keeps it), confirmedBy (who confirmed a proposal), supersededByReview (a precision review took it out of use), reinstatedAt (a later "correct" verdict applied it again).
- settlement-batches: name, reference=batchReference (the validator copies one to the other), status pending/reconciled/variance (derived by reconciliation, never set directly); data provider, batchReference, currency (the ISO 4217 code, in capitals, of the batch's money: a batch holds one currency, and grossKobo, feeKobo, netKobo, expectedFeeKobo, feeVarianceKobo and statementNetKobo hold that currency's smallest unit whatever their Kobo names say, kobo for naira and cents for USD), grossKobo, feeKobo (stated by the provider), expectedFeeKobo (what its lines' fees should be by the per-provider `providerFeeSchedule` in settings, default 0.5% capped at ₦1,000, each line by the schedule of the provider it names; see observations), feeVarianceKobo (feeKobo less expectedFeeKobo), netKobo, lineObservationIds, linePaymentIds, otherCurrencyLineIds (the settlement lines in another currency than the batch, linked to it as evidence and never counted), statementObservationId (the first bank statement credit in the batch's currency linked by its batch reference) and statementNetKobo (the sum of every credit in the batch's currency that names the batch, so a second credit is compared with the net total instead of replacing the first; a credit with the reference and amount of one already counted is the same bank line again, adds nothing and is marked duplicateStatementCredit), statementOtherCurrencies (the credits that name the batch in another currency, as `OtherCurrencies`: by code, how many and their amount in that currency's minor unit), explanation (with two or more credits it says how many the total sums), enteredTotals (the gross, fee and net Finance typed for a batch added by hand, and their currency, kept when the provider's settlement lines arrive and rebuild the totals), providerIdentityKey (the batch's identity, `[connection, batch reference]` with the connection compared without case or surrounding spaces, which a line or statement credit must share to join it), providerIdentityReview (the snapshot of a batch held for its provider identity: when, the identities it claimed, its evidence and its earlier status, totals and statement link) and providerIdentityRelease (when reconciliation released a held batch, as which identity, and the lines it held meanwhile, heldLineIds; for one released as the identity Finance confirmed, also who confirmed it and when, confirmedBy and confirmedAt, the exception that recorded it, exceptionId, the settlement lines and statement credits of other connections it gave up, detachedLineIds and detachedCreditIds, and the provider and providerConnection it named before when they named another connection, previous) and providerIdentityHistory (every release a later hold ended, oldest first, each as `{release, heldAgainAt, identities, observationIds}`: the release, when the batch was held again, and the identities and evidence it was held for then; it only grows) and providerIdentityClaimedBy (on a batch held for its provider identity: each identity it was held for that another batch records or claims, as `{identity, batchId, reference, handEntered}`, the other batch's id and reference and whether Finance entered it by hand; recorded at each reconciliation and absent when there is none; a confirmation as such an identity is refused, and the console offers only the others). Reconciliation records statementObservationId, statementNetKobo, statementOtherCurrencies, lineObservationIds, linePaymentIds, otherCurrencyLineIds, expectedFeeKobo, feeVarianceKobo, enteredTotals, providerIdentityKey, providerIdentityReview, providerIdentityRelease, providerIdentityHistory and providerIdentityClaimedBy; the record API cannot set or change them, and Finance corrects a batch through its totals (grossKobo, feeKobo and netKobo). A batch Finance adds by hand is in the currency it gives (data.currency, an ISO 4217 code with a minor unit, `currencyMinorUnit`, in any case; naira when left out; any other value is refused with 400), which Finance may correct while no provider line has built the batch; the currency of a batch the provider's lines build is its first counted line's, and an edit that changes it is refused (400). A batch an earlier build saved without a currency is in naira, unless its first counted line, or for a batch that counts none the first line linked to it, is in another currency, which the next reconciliation records; a batch that holds a line in another currency names its own from then on. Statement batch credits never become customer payments. FIN-03: a batch is one provider connection's payout. A settlement line joins the batch with its batch reference and its connection (its providerConnection, else its provider, else the lender's provider), and a new one of another connection makes a batch of its own; a statement credit is linked only to the batch of its own connection and batch reference, so a credit that names no connection matches the lender's own connection's batch, and a credit an earlier build linked to a batch stays linked unless it names another connection than the batch's. A batch is held for its provider identity only when it is genuinely ambiguous: the evidence linked to it names more than one connection (each line's and credit's providerConnection, else provider, with the identity the batch records and, for a batch Finance added by hand, the provider it records, when it records one), or another batch claims its identity. Evidence that names no connection carries no identity and never makes a batch ambiguous. A held batch is in variance with a Finance-owned settlement_variance exception of its own (condition `settlement_variance:<batch id>:provider_identity`), which concerns only the hold: reconciliation creates it for every held batch that has none for its current hold (open, or decided since the hold began other than as its condition cleared), even while another settlement_variance is open for the batch, and it never carries another report (a report of a collection counted in two batches, or of a line in another currency, goes to the batch's other open exception, or one of its own). Its totals and statement link are kept as they were, nothing recounts it, and a later line of a connection it was held for is linked to it (settlementBatchId, providerIdentityHeld on the line) but not counted, until Finance or an administrator confirms whose payout it is; reconciling or importing again does not release it. Once the providers have confirmed it, Finance or an administrator resolves the hold's exception as provider_identity_confirmed, its only code, naming one of the identities the batch was held for (see `resolve_exception`), and the next reconciliation releases the batch as that identity, once, before it counts any line; only a confirmation decided since the batch's current hold began counts, and one whose identity another batch records or claims, as the batches stand before that reconciliation decides anything, is not applied: a confirmation does not settle a collision between two batches. The batch then keeps an open exception for its hold, a new one since a decision is never reopened, with a dated line naming the other batch and saying the data owner corrects the duplicate batch's reference or provider; once no other batch claims the identity, the confirmation applies at the next reconciliation. Its evidence of that connection, and evidence that names no connection, stays with it; each settlement line of another connection leaves its lines and, while its totals are the ones its lines added, its gross, fee and net (totals Finance typed by hand stay as typed), and always its expected fee, which only reconciliation records, with its fee variance recomputed, and is read as a new line is, in its own connection's batch; each statement credit of another connection is unlinked from it, to link to its own connection's batch, and its statement link and total are summed again from the credits it keeps; and the lines it held meanwhile are counted as a new line would be. A batch that recorded no identity takes the confirmed one as providerIdentityKey and its connection as provider and providerConnection. The released batch's status follows its totals and statement link again, and another batch claiming the same identity does not hold it again; the reconciliation's answer counts it among settlementProviderIdentityReleases, and its audit entry names the batch, the identity, who confirmed it and when, and each line and credit that moved. Reconciling again changes nothing more. Where the providers cannot attribute the payout to one connection, the hold's exception stays open until the data owner repairs the evidence; once the batch's evidence names one connection, which no other batch claims, the next reconciliation releases it as described below and closes the exception as its condition cleared. A batch still held whose hold's exception an earlier build resolved with another code since the hold began, or closed in any way without a confirmed identity, gets a renewed review of that decision at the next reconciliation, once: a Finance-owned settlement_variance named Review earlier settlement identity decision (condition `settlement_variance:<batch id>:provider_identity:review:<earlier exception id>`, `providerIdentityReviewCondition`), whose notes say that the earlier resolution keeps its meaning but that the batch stays held until Finance or an administrator confirms whose payout it is, and which offers and accepts only provider_identity_confirmed, from Admin or Finance only. No second review of the same decision is raised, open or resolved; the release follows a confirmation recorded on either exception, and the reconciliation's answer counts the reviews it raised (settlementProviderIdentityReviews) and its audit entry names each. A released batch is held again when its linked evidence later names another connection, as only an edit outside reconciliation can make it, or when another batch claims its identity, as a copy Finance entered by hand with the same reference and provider did under an earlier build, unless Finance confirmed the batch's identity, which another batch's claim does not undo: its release moves to providerIdentityHistory with when it was held again, a confirmation of an earlier hold does not decide the new one, and the new hold gets an exception of its own. A batch saved before identities were recorded takes the one connection its evidence names, else the lender's own, never the connection an earlier build copied to it from its first payment, and records it as providerIdentityKey at the next reconciliation. A held batch whose evidence names one connection, which no other batch claims, such as one an earlier build held although it was not ambiguous or one whose evidence the data owner has repaired, is released at the next reconciliation, once: providerIdentityRelease is recorded beside its snapshot, its hold's exception, and a renewed review of it, close as their condition cleared, except a hold exception an earlier build made carry a report of a collection counted in two batches or of a line in another currency, which stays open for that report with a dated line saying so and, the batch no longer being held, offers the other settlement_variance codes; the lines it held, never counted, are read as new lines are (in it, or in the batch of their own connection), and its status follows its kept totals and statement link again, as before the hold; the reconciliation's answer counts such batches (settlementProviderIdentityReleases) and its audit entry names each. Every reconciliation and daily close re-derives the status from the batch's current totals: pending until a statement credit is linked, unless the fees already differ from the schedule by more than ₦100; reconciled when the credit equals netKobo and the fees are within ₦100; variance otherwise, with a settlement_variance exception. Decision on currencies: the fees are checked only where a fee schedule exists for the batch's currency (`hasFeeSchedule`; every schedule, `providerFeeSchedule`, the legacy providerFeeBps and the plan's default, is in naira, `FEE_SCHEDULE_CURRENCY`), so a batch in another currency has no expectedFeeKobo, no feeVarianceKobo and no feeSchedule, is never in variance for its fees, and its explanation says so whatever its state ("Its fees were not checked, because there is no fee schedule for USD.", after "It waits for its statement credit." while it is pending); its statement check still runs. A credit in another currency cannot match the batch’s amount after fees in NGN.") and its exception's condition ends with each such currency and amount (`settlement_variance:<batch id>:statement:<statementNetKobo or none>:<netKobo>:<feeKobo>:USD=2487500`). A line imported after the credit matched, such as the second part of a provider file split across two imports, or an edit to the totals, moves the batch at the next reconciliation. A line adds its gross, its fee (stated, else its gross less what it paid out) and the schedule's fee on its gross. A line that states only what it paid out, for a payment whose gross is not yet known, adds that payout as its net: with a stated fee the two make its gross, and without one it adds no fee and no expected fee, so a gross not yet known never puts its batch in variance. When the debit's own gross later completes that payment, the batch that counts the line takes the line's gross and fee as they now are and the schedule's fee on that gross, and the next evaluation follows its totals. A line an earlier build counted before its gross was known, as a gross of its payout with the schedule's fee taken off it, is counted again the same way, once, at the first reconciliation that finds its payment's gross known, while the batch's totals are still the ones its lines added; a batch Finance corrected by hand keeps the totals Finance typed. A batch holds one currency: its first counted line's. A settlement line in another currency than its batch is never added to the batch's totals: it is linked to the batch as evidence (settlementBatchId, and the batch's otherCurrencyLineIds), marked otherCurrencyLine, and reported to Finance with a settlement_variance exception of its own on the batch that names the batch, the line and both currencies, in the line's currency (condition `settlement_variance:<batch id>:currency:<line id>`, amountKobo the line's payment in that currency's minor unit, data.currency when that is not naira; when an exception is already open for the batch, that exception carries the report as a dated line and in otherCurrencyLines), which stays open until Finance resolves it; the payment itself is reconciled as any payment is. A batch an earlier build saved with lines in several currencies is corrected once, at the next reconciliation: each counted line in another currency than its first line's is taken out of its lines and, while the batch's totals are still the ones its lines added, out of its totals, and is reported the same way (its exception's notes say what the earlier build did, and that totals Finance typed by hand are left as typed); a batch in a currency with no fee schedule loses the expected fee and fee variance the naira schedule gave it and its lines; the reconciliation's answer counts the lines taken out (settlementLinesSeparated) and its audit entry names each. When no batch counts the collection of a line taken out any more, the provider's other lines of it, which the earlier build reported as counted in that batch (countedInBatchId), are read again in order as a new line is: the first in a batch that counts no line yet, or lines in its currency, is counted there (a batch that counts none takes its currency); one in a batch of another currency becomes a line in another currency there; and the rest are counted twice with the batch that now counts the collection. Each open report gains a dated line saying so, the report of the line taken out names the batch that now counts its collection, and so does the audit entry. A collection is counted in one batch only: a settlement line for a payment another batch already counts adds nothing to its own batch, is marked duplicateSettlementLine with countedInBatchId, and raises a settlement_variance exception on its batch that names both batches (condition `settlement_variance:<batch id>:line:<line id>`; when an exception is already open for the batch, that exception carries the report as a dated line and in countedTwice), so a collection paid out twice shows as that batch's variance once its statement credit arrives. A later batch that already lists a collection an earlier batch counts, as an earlier build could leave it, raises a settlement_variance exception that names both batches at the next reconciliation, once per collection (condition `settlement_variance:<batch id>:counted:<payment id>`; carried by the batch's exception, as a dated line and in countedTwice, when one is already open); its totals are left as they are for Finance to correct. Each time the batch moves while an exception for it is open, the exception's notes gain a dated line saying where the batch now stands (`Update on 2027-07-02 (WAT): the batch is now reconciled.` followed by the batch's explanation, or, for a pending batch, that it waits for its statement credit), so the text it was raised with is not read as current. An exception raised for the batch's statement credit or its fees closes as its condition cleared once the batch is no longer in variance (reconciled, or pending with fees within the schedule), in the reconciliation or action that finds it so; one raised for an earlier variance stays open while the batch is in another, and one raised for, or carrying a report of, a collection counted in two batches or a line in another currency than the batch stays open for Finance to resolve (its notes say so once the batch leaves variance), and the close review lists it until then. A report of a line counted in two batches that an earlier build closed with its batch's variance is raised again on its own at the next reconciliation. An earlier build carried such a report on the batch's open exception as a dated line only: while that exception is open, the next reconciliation lists the report in its countedTwice, so it stays open and its resolution settles the report; once Finance has resolved it, whose resolution replaced those notes with Finance's reason, the report is settled and is not raised again.

Synthetic does NOT pass real Test5.
- evidence: status pending/recorded; data gateId (P1…P5, F1…F4, T1b, T2; what the gate register matches on), reference, notes. Evidence registers are not verification.
- experiments: draft/preregistered/closed; data baselineRate, holdoutShare (0.1–0.5), minPerArm (a floor for both groups), analysisDate, enrolmentClose, seed, policyId; preregistration adds sampleCalculation {holdoutMinimum, engineMinimum}, each group's own minimum. analysisDate and enrolmentClose are WAT dates: enrolment takes a first failure up to the end of the enrolmentClose day in WAT, and the analysis date is reached at midnight WAT.
- costs: name infrastructure/support/notifications; amountKobo, data period.
- calendar: name holiday, data date. Created or changed only by Admin and Operations (403 for other roles).
- members: read-only demo role identities.

- `/operations` lists this caller's received requests for the selected lender. Replays use the stored original request under current permissions: the journal keeps a keyed write's method, path and whole body as sent, fields no route reads included (up to the 2 MB and 10,000-value body limits, sealed with the other payloads when payload encryption is on), because a retry sends that body through its route's current checks again; a repeat with the key is compared with the body's fingerprint. Cancellation blocks later execution only when no completion receipt exists. A cancelled request can no longer complete, even if an earlier attempt with its key was still running. A repeat sent while another attempt with its key is running (a double submission, a retry after a lost answer, Check original request) is turned away as still running and leaves the request to finish; a repeat's own failure never cancels the entry, which only the attempt that created it, while no other attempt runs it, or a definitive refusal closes. Each entry says what it asked (`summary`, `OperationSummary`): the action or route in plain words, the kind and ID of the record it names, and at most three short fields it named (an action, a decision, a status, a kind or a format). A connected-banking permission opens on Permissions & readiness (`/connections`). For an API client, `summary` is a new property present on every entry, and a completed export's `recordKind` used to be the kind it exported (`customers` for a customer register): a client that followed it to a record of that kind now gets `exports`, the kind of the record `recordId` names. `GET /v1/operations/pending` counts the caller's pending entries in the lender, and the navigation's Operations link shows that count, so a person who reloads after losing an answer sees that a request waits there. No tooltip provider sits at the root, since nothing uses tooltips.
- Internationalisation (`lib/formatters.ts`; the audit in the design rationale): words and dates in British English (`<html lang="en-GB">`), numbers and money in Nigerian conventions (₦25,000.00), and every instant in West Africa Time with the zone named ("18 Sept 2026, 13:50 WAT"), whatever the device is set to; a value that is only a day (YYYY-MM-DD) shows as a day; counts go through `formatCount` (the API's `counted` from the schema package) so the noun agrees with the number, and every other count, total and percentage the console shows goes through `formatNumber` or `formatPercent` (ratios at a fixed number of places; basis points and whole percentages with only the places they need), so a large figure is grouped (1,234) wherever it appears; `formatKobo` splits kobo into naira and kobo with integer arithmetic, so every amount up to the largest safe integer (₦90,071,992,547,409.91) shows to the exact kobo; list searches fold case and marks on both sides (`foldForSearch` in the API), so "obi" finds "Ọbi"; the synthetic customers carry their Yoruba and Igbo marks.
- Observability (`docs/observability.md`): every API answer carries `X-Request-Id` and every error body `requestId`; `saidBy` in `lib/notify.tsx` ends a problem notice for a 5xx with "Reference <id>" (`referenceOf` reads the body, then the header) and the workspace failure page gives the time and the reference together; a refusal stays in the rule's own words. `GET /api/healthz` (build, uptime, scheduler, whose optional `backlog` counts the lenders whose scheduled close is failing or overdue without naming any, as the process last read them, null until its first read, with public anonymous sandboxes counted apart in its optional `publicSandboxes`; with the `external` state the process reads it itself, every `intervalMs`; an answer from an earlier build has no `backlog`, or no `publicSandboxes`, or with `external` a null `backlog` and `intervalMs`; and the optional `background` worker observations, whose cleanup `lastResult` counts files waiting for a retry, `pendingFailures`, and apart from them files parked for an operator's review because their stored identity did not match, `parked`, which an earlier build's answer does not have) and `GET /api/readyz` (database round trip on its own connection that also checks the tables, columns, unique indexes and check constraints this build needs, and that no guard it replaced remains, 503 while either fails) need no sandbox, sign-in or Clerk, and allow 120 checks a minute per client network.
- Busy answers (`artifacts/api-server/src/lib/database-limits.ts`): when a lender or the workspace is busy, or the database does not answer within its limits (a lock wait, a statement, an idle or lost connection, no free connection), any operation may answer 503 with `Retry-After` in seconds, `error` in plain words and, when nothing was saved, `committed: false`. For a request with an Idempotency-Key that is decided for the key, not for the attempt: `committed: false` only when nothing sent with the key was or can be saved (its journal entry is cancelled, or nothing was saved under the key before the request failed). A repeat of a saved request is answered with its stored result before its lender is loaded, so it neither loads nor waits for a busy lender. A repeat of a saved request that cannot be answered that way (its stored result cannot be opened), or of one still running, never says nothing was saved: it names the key's journal entry (`operation: "completed"`, `"running"`, or `"pending"` while its outcome is not confirmed) and says so in its words, such as "This lender is busy with another change. This request was saved. Try again in a moment." A repeat sent while another attempt with its key is running is answered at once with 503, `Retry-After: 2` and `operation: "running"` ("This request is still running."), and leaves the entry to that attempt. Queries retry a 503 (the query client's default retries). A mutation treats one with `committed: false` as not saved (`nothingSaved` in `lib/safe-mutations.ts`), so the next submission uses a new key, and holds any other as unconfirmed, to be retried with the same key. A journaled request's entry is closed only by a definitive refusal, or by a failure of the attempt that created it while no other attempt runs it. The contract lists this 503, with `Retry-After`, for every operation that can answer it. A read answers from one snapshot of the database and takes no lender lock: it never waits for a lender's writes, and during one (a month-end close, say) it shows the state from before it. A tenant (a sandbox or a staff organisation) holds at most a third of a process's connections for its lenders and one lender at most half of that; a request past its share waits without a connection and, after 5 s, is answered 503 (`lender_busy`).
- Refusals and failures (`lib/api-spec/openapi.json`, `ErrorBody`): each operation lists the statuses its route can answer, and every one carries the same body (except readiness's own 503 and the identity provider's re-verification instruction): `error` in plain words and `requestId`, with `details` (one `field` and `message` for each parameter, header or body field the request contract refused, at most 20, and `detailCount`, how many problems there were), `code` (why pilot access was refused), `committed: false` (nothing was saved) and `operation` (the state of the request's journal entry: `completed`, `running`, `pending` or `cancelled`, the last meaning the entry is closed) when they apply. A read that fails is a 500 saying the service could not prepare the answer, without `committed`: a read saves nothing either way. Every POST and PATCH lists 400, 413 and 415, because the body parser reads a body even where the operation takes none: a body is UTF-8 JSON sent uncompressed as `application/json`, and one in any other format, a form's included, or another character set is refused (415). A compressed body (any `Content-Encoding` but `identity`) is refused before it is read, with 415 and `Accept-Encoding: identity` (the Paystack test ingress alone reads gzip, deflate and br). A GET's or HEAD's body is ignored, never read. A body is at most 2 MB, nested at most 32 levels deep and holds at most 10,000 values, every object, list, text, number, true, false and null counted; the last two are counted from the body's bytes, before it is parsed. Before anything is journaled or saved, a body nested more than 32 levels deep, or holding a NUL character or an unpaired UTF-16 surrogate in a string or a field name, is refused (400, naming the field), one holding more than 10,000 values is refused (413), and so is a path whose percent-encoding cannot be decoded (400). A path after `/api` is matched exactly, in its case and without a trailing slash: another spelling is an unknown resource (404), never journaled. A service a request depends on that cannot be reached (private storage, the identity provider) is a 503 with `Retry-After`. Every write whose Idempotency-Key the journal records lists 410, the answer to a repeat of a completed request whose stored result the lender's retention policy has removed; the export download and retry list it for a file retention deleted. 429 and 503 carry `Retry-After` in seconds; a 429 says 60 after a request limit (300 a minute for a signed-in person, a sandbox the server has served or otherwise the client's network, an IPv4 address or IPv6 /64; 1,200 a minute per network; 120 health checks a minute per network), 3600 after a new-sandbox limit (per network, per IPv6 /48 or per server), and 30 when the lender already has ten exports waiting or running. An operation scoped to a lender answers a missing `merchantId`, or one sent twice, with the same 400 everywhere, naming the field in `details`; the generated query validators no longer turn a missing value into the text `undefined`, which some routes had answered as an unknown lender (404). Date and time inputs (every `expectedUpdatedAt`, a case's `nextActionAt`, a source profile's `firstExpectedAt` and a record list's `updatedSince`) accept `Z` or an offset, such as `2026-09-18T08:00:00+01:00`, from the year 0001 to 9999; the service stores and compares the UTC instant and answers it with `Z`. A date in a record (a day as YYYY-MM-DD, or a UTC timestamp such as `2026-09-18T07:00:00Z`) must be a real calendar date: `2026-02-30` or `2026-02-28T24:00:00Z` is refused (400), never moved to the next month or day. A record the request names that does not exist in the lender is a 404 on every route, whether the path names it or the body does (an action's `recordId`, a record's `customerId` or an exception's `linkedRecordId`, an export's close review, a retention run to execute). A path parameter that is refused is named in `details` (`id`, `kind`, `connectionId`), never as an empty field, and an id in the address is 1 to 100 characters on every route that takes one: a longer id is the same 400, naming `id`, everywhere, checked before the body. A record's name cannot be empty or blank (400); its `status` is at most 100 characters, its `reference` 200, its `customerId` 100 and an observation's `data.eventId` 200, and longer text is refused (400, naming the field) before anything is saved: such text used to reach a database index that could not hold it, a 500. A money calculation a request needs that cannot be done exactly within the supported amount and rate limits is refused with 422 and `code`: `INVALID_MONEY_AMOUNT`, `INVALID_MONEY_RATE` or `MONEY_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The operations that compute money list it: the overview, reports, a customer's timeline and history, the connected workspace and its actions, workspace actions and a retry from Operations. It is a definitive refusal, so a keyed request's journal entry is closed (`operation: "cancelled"`) and nothing waits in Operations. Before, such a refusal left the entry pending and named no `operation`, a Cash Desk total beyond the range answered 400 on its actions and 500 on `GET /v1/connected`, and `code` named only staff-access refusals.
- The journal's header and the sandbox cookie (`lib/api-spec/openapi.json`, `components.headers` and `components.securitySchemes`): every answer to a request whose Idempotency-Key the journal records, success, refusal or failure, carries `X-Valopay-Operation`, the id of its journal entry, which `GET /v1/operations` lists and `POST /v1/operations/{id}/retry` and `/cancel` take; it is absent without a key, when the key itself is refused and for a demo role switch, which the journal does not record. An anonymous sandbox is named by its cookie (`sandboxCookie`): a random token the service sets on the first visit and renews on every answer, HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax, for 30 days from the last visit, called `__Host-valopay_sandbox` behind TLS and `valopay_sandbox` on a plain-HTTP local run. A Paystack test delivery's signature covers the body's bytes as sent, or for a body sent with `Content-Encoding` gzip, deflate or br, the JSON bytes after decompression; another encoding is refused (415).
- Answers (`artifacts/api-server/src/lib/contract.ts`): every answer, including the ones the pilot, operations, team, readiness, sources, close review, correction, retention, work and connected routes build themselves, is checked against its schema before it is sent. The schemas for those routes live in `lib/valopay-schema` and the contract's components are generated from them. A write's answer is checked inside its transaction, before COMMIT, so an answer that does not match saves nothing: the caller gets a 500 in general words with `committed: false`, and the log records `response.invalid` with the paths that failed, never their values. The answers the retention, personal work and import correction builders check themselves are checked the same way, so a mismatch is never a validation 400 that blames the request; a timestamp stored with an offset, as an earlier build may have written it, is answered as its UTC instant. A repeat of a saved request (the same Idempotency-Key, or a retry from Operations) answers the stored receipt: fields an earlier build stored that the contract no longer lists are left out, and the log records a `response.invalid` warning; a receipt that cannot be answered within the contract is a 500 that says the request was saved (`operation: "completed"`), never `committed: false`, and so is a repeat whose stored receipt cannot be opened (the key service's 503). The console reads these answers through the same schemas (`lib/answers.ts`, `lib/pilot.ts`, `lib/connected.ts`): a field a newer service added is accepted, but a missing or malformed one is not, and an addition counts only within the shape the schema chose, never in one alternative of a union the answer might match by accident. A connected action's confirmation is read in the one shape its action gives (`connectedActionResultFor` in `lib/valopay-schema`, which the API checks it with too): a Cash Desk action's outcome, and for every other action the record it produced, of the lender the request named, so an outcome without a record never confirms a consent, checkout or credit action. A page then says the service's answer was incomplete instead of showing it, and a write is held as an incomplete confirmation, to be checked in Operations before it is submitted again.

## Mutations
`listRecords` takes status (for saved exports also `expired`, which no job stores: it lists the exports whose file an approved retention run deleted, whatever their job's status, and `ready` and `failed` then list only the exports whose file remains), search (the record's name, its reference and the text and number values in its data, nested ones included, ignoring case and accents; never a field's name, `true`, `false`, `null` or JSON's own quotes and braces, so a value holding a double quote is found as written and a word every record's keys share matches nothing), and for paging `limit` (1 to 500, a value outside that range refused (400); omitted returns the whole filtered set, newest first, except for the kinds that grow with history, `audit`, `closes`, `exports`, `notifications` and `retry-decisions`, whose list without a limit holds the newest 500 with `nextOffset` to page on), `offset` and `updatedSince` (an RFC 3339 date and time with `Z` or an offset, such as `2026-09-18T08:00:00+01:00`, inclusive, for incremental sync; a number, a date without a time, a time without `Z` or an offset, or an impossible date is refused (400, naming `updatedSince`)); the response carries items, total (the filtered count) and nextOffset when more rows remain. For instalments, `allocatable=true` lists only those that can take an allocation now: they still owe an amount (the outstanding balance, or the whole amount before there is one), are not cancelled, closed or in dispute, and are not held for a renewed review of an earlier reversal decision (a non-empty data.legacyReversalReviewIds, whatever their status), which is what `manual_allocate` accepts (`canTakeAllocation` in the shared schema; a hold the service derives before the first reconciliation records it is refused only when the allocation is sent). With `paymentId` it lists only the instalments a manual allocation of that payment accepts, by the payer rule the allocation applies (`allocationPayer`): a payment with a recorded payer takes only its payer's instalments, one whose evidence named no payer but names an instalment takes only that instalment's customer's, and one that names neither takes any customer's; a payment in another currency than naira, whose money went back, with nothing left to allocate or that reconciliation holds for a renewed review of an earlier reversal decision (data.legacyReversalReviewIds) takes none, so the list is empty; a hold the service derives before the first reconciliation records it is refused only when the allocation is sent. A payment the lender does not have is a 404, and `paymentId` without `allocatable=true` is refused (400). Its total is therefore the number of choices, and the allocation picker asks for it with the payment it allocates, so its pager counts exactly the instalments it offers and a page never comes back with choices it would hide or that the allocation would refuse; with none it says so in a plain line instead of a pager. `allocatable=true` with any other kind is refused (400); omitted or `false`, every instalment is listed. A list of closes has each close as its summary, as the reports have earlier closes: its data without `operational` and `metrics`, and its report reduced to `unallocated` and `exceptions`; a search of closes matches that summary, and `GET /v1/close-history/{id}` returns a close whole. createRecord and updateRecord for editable kinds. Business actions go to performAction:
- `set_role` data.role (Admin / Operations / Finance / Compliance reviewer / Read-only), **demo personas ONLY**, each is a separate simulated identity; no real permission granted. Like a team change, it waits only for the requests already running in the workspace, later requests wait behind it, and it answers 503 with `Retry-After` when it cannot start within 5 s: with `committed: false` without an Idempotency-Key, and otherwise as not confirmed, retried with the same key, since the key may have saved an earlier switch. Its answer is kept apart from the journal's, so the same key sent with a journaled write is a separate request.
- `kill_switch` data.enabled boolean, optional data.policyId, and reason; requires Admin. Cancels scheduled attempts under the switched scope. Turning a stop on is immediate and settles any waiting request to lift it. In a staff pilot, turning it off saves a request instead (settings.emergencyStopReleases, keyed `lender` or `policy:<id>`, with requestedBy, requestedAt, reason and policyId) and the stop stays on: result.data releaseRequested true. The sandbox, one person playing every role, turns it off at once.
- `approve_kill_switch_off` optional data.policyId, and reason; requires Admin. Approves the waiting request to lift that stop: an administrator other than the one who asked (403), and a request must be waiting (409). Turns the stop off; result.data requestedBy, requestedAt and auditNote, which the audit entry adds to the reason.
- `request_instruction` always blocks: live integration/security gates unmet.
- `mandate_suspend` (active only), `mandate_reinstate` (suspended only), `mandate_cancel` (cancels scheduled attempts), `mandate_reissue` (creates a new mandate; needs data.consentEvidence; optional data.amountKobo, the limit the new consent covers, whole kobo above 0, the current limit when absent), `activation_reminder` (pending_activation only; caps 4 transfer_to_activate / 2 hosted_consent, none once consentGiven; refused in quiet hours 21:00–08:00 WAT; logs a notifications record) recordId and reason.
- `submit_policy`, `approve_policy`, `reject_policy`, `new_policy_version` recordId and reason; approval uses different actor and Compliance reviewer role. Same for templates via `submit_template`, `approve_template`. `new_policy_version` numbers the draft one above the highest version of the policy, so two drafts from one version never share a number, and `approve_policy` is refused (409) while another approved version of the same policy (linked by previousVersionId, or sharing its name) carries that number. Templates have no such refusal: `new_template_version` numbers its draft after the template's whole history, and `approve_template` does not check the number.
- `run_reconciliation`, `daily_close` no id. Reconciliation resolves observations to canonical payments, holds suspected duplicates (a payment for an already-paid due item, or the same payer and amount within two minutes), applies the ladder R1 to R5, re-evaluates every settlement batch against its fee schedule (where one exists for its currency) and linked statement credit, re-derives instalment statuses that contradict their stored balance (`dueStatusesRepaired`), raises catalogue exceptions with business-day deadlines, evaluates every open obligation under an approved policy and records the decision (RET-03), applies the 6.3 give-up rows (unpaid_final, in_dispute, unknown outcome after 24 hours) and raises notice_not_evidenced when a planned attempt's notice deadline passes without provider acceptance. A provider reversal of a payment that was applied reopens each instalment it paid in dispute, with an Operations-owned customer_dispute exception naming the reversal (one already open for the instalment gains it as a dated line). A pay-by-bank checkout whose outcome has been unknown for 24 hours raises a Finance-owned unknown_outcome exception linked to it (`checkoutOutcomesUnknown`). An exception an earlier build raised for money in another currency, with that money's minor units in amountKobo and no currency, is given the currency of the money it is about, once, resolved or not (`exceptionCurrenciesRecorded`, present when there were some). A settlement line an earlier build counted in a batch of another currency is taken out of it, once, and reported (`settlementLinesSeparated`, present when there were some; see settlement-batches). A settlement batch whose evidence is genuinely ambiguous is held for its provider identity (`settlementProviderIdentityHolds`, present while some are), and one an earlier build held that is not, or one whose identity Finance or an administrator confirmed, is released (`settlementProviderIdentityReleases`); a batch still held whose hold's exception an earlier build closed without a confirmed identity gets a renewed review, once (`settlementProviderIdentityReviews`; see settlement-batches). A waiting reversal whose resolution an earlier build recorded without its rules is held for a renewed Finance review (`legacyReversalReviewsPending`, present while some are open), and the instalments such a hold paused, or an earlier build's hold put in dispute, get their status back (`legacyReversalStatusesRestored`; see `resolve_exception`). The audit entry names each. Last, every open exception whose condition cleared is closed (`exceptionsCleared`; result.data.auditNote names them). `daily_close` additionally writes the REC-07 close report on a `closes` record (result.data.closeId, positionAlert, schedule) as a manual close, with optional data.sourceBusinessDate (today or earlier in WAT). The scheduled close (REC-01) is not an action: while its scheduler is enabled and running, the API process checks each lender's requested `closeTime` (default 07:00 WAT) with the system actor `System · scheduled close`. Each scheduled time closes one business date, the WAT day before it (the 07:00 close of 29 June closes 28 June), and checks that date's source files. `settings.nextCloseAt` is the oldest scheduled time still owed: a close that starts at or after it covers that time alone (a scheduled close always; a manual one unless it names another source business date) and moves it one day on. After an outage every missed business date therefore gets its own catch-up close, oldest first, each with its own source evidence and Finance review; the scheduler runs one per lender per pass and the lender stays due until none is owed. A manual close's source business date is the one it names, else the owed date it covers, else its WAT run date. While the automatic close is off nothing is owed and a close leaves `nextCloseAt` alone. The result's message names the business date a close covered and how many missed dates are still owed, and the missed-close alert names those dates, with their number as its count. `updateSettings` accepts `closeTime` (HH:MM WAT) and `scheduledCloseEnabled`; a changed time or switching the preference on recomputes `nextCloseAt` from now, and an unchanged save leaves a pending missed close pending. The preference does not start the scheduler. A failed scheduled attempt is retried with a growing delay and the close stays pending until one succeeds; any close clears the retry. The scheduler switches off the automatic close of an anonymous sandbox nobody has changed for seven days instead of closing it. `getOverview` carries `closeSchedule`, `closeTime` and legacy `nextClose`, which is empty whenever an automatic run is unavailable.
- Payment evidence and the matching ladder, as `run_reconciliation` and every close apply them. Evidence resolves to one canonical payment by its key: the provider connection it came through and its reference. Evidence whose key finds no payment while a payment with its reference was observed through another connection is held: it stays unresolved with a Finance-owned suspected_duplicate exception that names both connections, and any conflict with that payment. Held for its connection alone, it can be resolved as the same payment: the next reconciliation joins it to that payment as more evidence of it (resolutionKey joined_after_review) and keys the payment under that connection too, so later evidence through either name finds it. Resolved as distinct payments or a confirmed duplicate, it becomes a payment of its own through its connection, so the same reference through another connection is another payment. A payment an earlier build keyed on its evidence's provider is also found through the connections its evidence named. Evidence under a payment's key that names another payer (or names a payer while the payment is tied to another customer's instalment), is in another currency or states another gross amount is never merged into that payment and never dropped: it stays unresolved with a Finance-owned suspected_duplicate exception linked to it (see `resolve_exception`). Evidence that names no payer agrees with any payer, and a settlement line that states only its net amount agrees with any gross at least that large; the debit's own gross then completes the payment, whatever of it is applied, unless its money went back (a refund or a reversal): the amount rises to the gross and the difference is unapplied money for Finance. Evidence of a reversal never becomes a payment of its own. Reported for a payment no connection has seen, it waits unresolved for that payment, with no exception at first: once it has waited 24 hours from the time its evidence gives (else from when it was recorded), a Finance-owned provider_status_mismatch exception linked to it (linkedKind observations) says the provider reported a reversal of a payment the platform has not seen; when that payment appears, the reversal applies through its own key or is held for the other connection's payment, and the exception closes as its condition cleared. A resolution Finance has recorded for evidence decides what happens to it before any payment is looked up, whatever order and pass the evidence and its payment arrive in (see `resolve_exception`): a held reversal resolved as the same payment is applied to that payment and reverses it, and resolved any other way it is set aside; a waiting reversal resolved as platform state confirmed is set aside, and resolved as provider state adopted it keeps waiting, with no new exception, and reverses its payment when that arrives, whichever spelling of the connection the payment comes through. Within a pass, evidence of a reversal is read after every other piece of evidence, so the order evidence arrives in within one import or close changes nothing. The ladder runs on unallocated payments. Money in another currency than NGN is never matched and is held with an unallocated_payment exception. R1 needs the attempt's debit reference or the evidence's instalment link, the lender's own connection (evidence through another connection is held with an unallocated_payment exception) and a gross amount equal to the instalment's; it applies the match automatically and with certainty only when the evidence names the payer, and otherwise proposes it (probable), so that Finance's confirmation identifies the payer. R2 and R3 read a dedicated virtual account. R4 reads instalment references in the narration on word boundaries, across the whole lender, and where references overlap the longest wins (a narration naming LN0042-10 names neither LN0042-1 nor LN0042); it applies only when the narration names exactly one instalment, which is the payer's, open and owed exactly the amount, so a narration naming several instalments, or a reference that two instalments share once case and punctuation are set aside, is never matched automatically. R5 proposes by amount, payer and a five-day window around the due date. A payment the ladder cannot apply is held for Finance with the reason and an unallocated_payment exception, so one record never stops the close. Unallocated payments and the unapplied rest of partial payments age into an unallocated_payment exception after 24 hours (an overpayment's excess has its overpayment exception); a payment with nothing unapplied is never matched and never ages.
- `confirm_allocation`, `reject_allocation` recordId=payment ID for proposal, reason, data `proposalId` and `proposalUpdatedAt` (both required, see above); Finance/Admin. Rejecting keeps whatever the payment has already applied elsewhere and records the pair in `rejectedDueItemIds`, so the next close does not propose it again. A proposal that no longer fits what the payment has left, for example after Finance allocated it elsewhere by hand, is withdrawn automatically. Confirming the proposal of a payment whose evidence named no payer identifies the payer, as `manual_allocate` does. Confirming is refused (409) while reconciliation holds the payment or its instalment for a renewed review of an earlier reversal decision (data.legacyReversalReviewIds), or before its first reconciliation records such a hold; the proposal links the two, so the refusal names the instalment. The console shows Confirm as unavailable, with that reason, for a recorded hold; rejecting stays available. A rejection withdraws a payer Finance identified, as `review_allocation` does, once the identifying allocation is out of use and nothing of the payment stays applied.
- `manual_allocate` recordId=payment ID, data.dueItemId and data.amountKobo, reason. Refused (409) for a payment or instalment reconciliation holds for a renewed review of an earlier reversal decision (data.legacyReversalReviewIds, or before its first reconciliation records the hold; the console shows Allocate as unavailable for a held payment, in the service's words, and `allocatable=true` does not list a held instalment), for a returned payment, for more than a payment refunded in part still holds, for a payment in another currency than NGN, and for an instalment of another customer than the payment's payer. Finance's own allocation to an instalment clears an earlier rejection of that pair. A payment whose evidence named no payer, such as the seeded SBX-UNIDENTIFIED-001 or a provider settlement line without a customer, is applied only here or by confirming its proposal, and never automatically: the allocation identifies the payer as the instalment's customer in the same action. The payment takes that customer, data.payerIdentification records who identified the payer, when, the reason, the instalment and the allocation, a proposal of the payment for another customer is withdrawn, and the answer carries data.payerCustomerId and data.auditNote. The audit entry's summary is the reason followed by that note, for example "Payer confirmed by phone. Payer identified as customer DEMO-C1005 for payment SBX-UNIDENTIFIED-001." It is refused (409) when the payment's own evidence names an instalment of another customer.
- `review_allocation` recordId=allocation ID, data.correct boolean, reason; Finance/Admin. `correct=false` supersedes the allocation, reopens the payment and due item (REC-09) and records the pair in `rejectedDueItemIds`, so later closes do not recreate the match. When the allocation that identified the payment's payer (payerIdentification.allocationId) is then out of use and nothing of the payment stays applied, `correct=false` also withdraws that identification: the payment has no payer again, a proposal of it for that customer is withdrawn too, payerIdentificationHistory keeps the identification with withdrawnBy, withdrawnAt and withdrawnReason, the message says so and data.auditNote ("Payer identification of customer DEMO-C1003 withdrawn for payment TRF-1: the match that identified the payer is out of use.") is added to the audit entry. Finance then applies the payment to its real payer's instalment with `manual_allocate`, which identifies that payer. A payer the evidence named is never withdrawn, nor is one that evidence resolved to the payment since names (the evidence confirms Finance's identification, and the repository refuses that withdrawal too), nor one whose payment's money went back. `correct=true` on a match a review had marked wrong applies the same allocation again, identifying its payer again, for the reviewer's reason, when that was withdrawn, or is refused (409) while the payment or instalment no longer allows it, including once the payment is recorded as another customer's; on a match superseded for another reason (a reversal) it records the verdict only. A proposal is refused (409): confirm or reject it instead. RecordDialog always submits checkbox fields as booleans.
- `release_dispute` recordId=due item, reason; Admin/Finance. Decision on leaving a dispute: takes an instalment out of in_dispute, whatever put it there, and its status follows its balance, except for a dispute a renewed reversal review's hold made (below). The release is recorded on the instalment (data.disputeRelease, via finance_release), and each open customer_dispute for it is closed as its condition cleared. A disputed debit the release covered does not put the instalment back in dispute: the retry engine records the stop decision dispute_released instead, since a disputed debit is never retried automatically, while a later disputed debit is a new dispute. Refused (409) for an instalment not in dispute, and for one a renewed reversal review holds (data.legacyReversalReviewIds, or before its first reconciliation records the hold; the console shows Release from dispute as unavailable for a recorded hold, in the service's words, and for a hold not yet recorded shows the service's refusal). Decision on a dispute such a hold made: the release returns the instalment to the status it had before the hold (legacyReversalReviewPause, or for one an earlier build's hold put in dispute, the status its records establish: paid, unpaid_final once the retry engine gave up on it, else its balance's), never to collection by default. result.data.dueStatus; the audit entry records the release and the exceptions it closed. Collection and allocation resume: a payment waiting for the instalment is matched at the next reconciliation or allocated by Finance, and pay-by-bank accepts it again.

A resolution keeps the meaning Finance was shown when it was recorded: `resolve_exception` records the rules it follows on the exception (data.resolutionRuleVersion, `resolutionRuleVersion`, now 1; the record API cannot set or change it), and only a resolution that records them follows the codes above. A held payment takes no allocation, confirmation or automatic match, and a held instalment no allocation, release from dispute, pay-by-bank checkout or retry plan (409, or the reversal_review decision). Refresh the exception to see where it stands.", checked before the codes, so a person whose dialog was open while the batch was released is told to refresh). - `record_refund` recordId=payment ID data.reference external refund reference, reason. Only records external action, never moves funds. A refund returns what the payment has not applied: with nothing applied the payment becomes `returned`; with money applied, such as an overpayment whose excess went back, that money stays on its instalments and the payment becomes `allocated`. Either way its proposals are withdrawn and nothing it has left can be allocated or counted as credit, until an allocation of the money that stayed is superseded. The amount returned is recorded as data.refundedKobo, in the payment's own minor unit, and given as result.data.refundedKobo and in the message in the payment's own currency (for example NGN 5,000.00, or USD 1,000.00 for a payment in dollars); billing and the uplift report keep the collection on the money that stayed. - `verify_audit` result.data.valid, count, headHash: the whole stored chain, checked from its first entry, and the lender records how far it held: its last verified entry, always before the first entry that breaks the chain, and the break, or none once the chain is valid. The check takes the entries in one order whatever a stored sequence holds: whole-number sequences by value, then every entry whose sequence is anything else (null, text, a fraction, zero or missing), each of which is itself a break where the check reaches it. So an entry whose sequence was damaged is found at the place it left in the chain, and never hides a fork. A sequence two entries claim (a fork) breaks the chain before that sequence, so neither entry is verified and headHash is the hash of the entry before them. The overview checks only the entries after the last verified one, so a change to an earlier entry shows there once `verify_audit` has found it; a missing latest entry (the stored head further on than any entry), a fork, a changed entry or a damaged sequence among the entries it reads shows at once (a damaged entry there is named after the entries that check verified, and `verify_audit` then names its place). Once a completed write, `verify_audit` or the daily check has recorded a break, the lender keeps it: the overview's alert stays on, naming the same entry, after any later write and after a repair, and each save and overview checks only the entries appended since the chain's head, so a broken chain costs them no more than a valid one however far it runs after the break. The overview is a read and records nothing, and nor does a write that is refused or rolled back: a break only they have found clears if the chain is repaired before one of them records it. Only a walk of the whole chain reads every entry again: `verify_audit`, or the daily check that the background worker runs for each lender once its first daily close of each West Africa Time day has committed, scheduled, one-shot or by a person (a close pass runs its checks after its closes; a pass that catches up several missed business dates checks once). The daily check walks the chain without holding the lender, so no request waits for it, then stores what it found as `verify_audit` does, except what is newer than its walk: a break a write recorded meanwhile at an entry the walk had read intact, and whatever `verify_audit` recorded from a walk that began reading after the daily check's did, stay. Either one records the break it finds or, once the chain is valid again, clears the break and the alert. The daily check appends no audit entry: the lender's settings record when it last ran (`settings.dailyAuditCheckAt`) and the log its outcome (`audit.daily_check`).
- `issue_invoice` data.period optional (YYYY-MM, default the previous month), reason; Admin/Finance. Periods are calendar months in West Africa Time: a collection at 00:30 WAT on 1 January belongs to January, and the default is the previous WAT month. Issues the immutable invoice for the period: the contracted licence of the signed terms in effect that month (see commercial), one usage line per billable collection not billed before and observed by the period end (so a collection withheld inside its reversal window is billed on the next invoice), the design-partner discount on the licence and this invoice's usage lines, the BIL-07 adjustment lines for collections billed earlier, each at the rate of the invoice that first billed its collection, the gated recovery fee, VAT on the net. Every month is invoiced, in order, and a month with nothing to bill gets a zero invoice: a month passed over could never be invoiced afterwards, and its licence would be lost. So a period after the next one due is refused, naming the month to issue first: the month after the latest invoice or, before the first invoice, the month the earliest signed terms took effect. Also refuses a second invoice for a period, a period before the latest invoice, a future period and the current month, which can be invoiced from 00:00 WAT on the 1st of the next month (all 400). ## Imports & exports

`/exports` lists saved evidence jobs for the selected lender, including processing stage, recovery status, permitted retry and private download. Its status filter offers Waiting, Preparing, Completed, Needs retry and File expired (`status=expired`): a job whose file retention removed is listed under File expired only, never under Completed or Needs retry. Stalled-export alerts lead here. The Sources business-date declaration and Import batches correction workbench preserve original evidence and require named Finance review for accepted source gaps and committed-record amendments.

The CSV console requires an explicit source amount unit for monetary record types. Naira means the major unit of each row's currency (`csvAmountToKobo`, `majorToMinor`): for a row that names no currency it accepts at most two decimal places (including correctly grouped quoted commas), and a row of payment evidence that names another currency is read with that currency's decimals by ISO 4217 (`currencyMinorUnit`: two for USD, none for JPY, three for KWD), so JPY 1000 is stored as 1000 and KWD 1.5 as 1500, and more decimals than its currency has, or a code ISO 4217 gives no minor unit, is a row error. Only payment evidence, the one kind with a `currency` field (`importFieldsOf`), reads a row's currency: in a file of another kind a column headed, or mapped to, currency is kept as extra detail and does not change how its amounts are read, and a batch's source total reads them the same way. Kobo means the minor unit and requires whole digits, whatever the row's currency; an amount that is not one is a row error naming kobo, or the smallest unit of the row's currency, and the other unit as the import screens call it for the kind ("Enter the smallest unit of USD as a whole number … Choose Major units (₦, or the row's currency) if the source gives amounts in USD rather than its smallest unit."). The import screens call the two units major and minor units where a row can name its own currency (`amountUnitName`), and naira and kobo elsewhere; forms write a stored amount in its currency's major unit with `minorToMajor`. Conversion uses decimal integer arithmetic and rejects negative, ambiguous or unsafe amounts. The server performs the same conversion for preview and commit. The first ten parsed rows include each row's converted amount (`amountKobo`, in the smallest unit of the row's currency), which the quick import's preview and Import batches' converted amounts show in that currency (Import batches finds the currency column from the batch's saved mapping, as the service does), and changing the unit or mapping invalidates the checked preview. A saved batch's source totals (`SourceBatchQuality`) never add currencies together: `sourceAmountKobo` and `importedAmountKobo` sum the naira rows only, and `sourceOtherCurrencies` and `importedOtherCurrencies` list each other currency's rows and amount in its minor unit beside them, only when there are some; Import batches and Sources show both ("₦15.00, JPY 1,000 (1 row) and USD 10.00 (1 row) source total"). A profile's expected amount and a declared file's total are in naira and are compared with the naira total only; a batch with rows in another currency whose profile expects an amount needs review ("The source profile's expected amount is in naira, so it is compared with the naira rows only; …"), which holds back its commit, and a declared file with such rows is incomplete, saying which money no declared total covers, with that money in `receivedOtherCurrencies`. **Change for API clients:** for a batch with rows in another currency, `sourceAmountKobo` and `importedAmountKobo` used to add every row's smallest unit whatever its currency (JPY 1,000 and USD 10.00 made 2000); they now hold the naira rows alone. A batch committed by an earlier build keeps the totals it was committed with, which may add rows in other currencies, and a declared file is compared with that total; Sources and the completeness panel say so. Existing API clients that omit `amountUnit` retain integer-kobo semantics. A blank cell in an optional number column is left out, not read as 0; the amount stays required for every record type except customers, and payment evidence must be for more than ₦0. The generic source header `amount` maps to the canonical amount field; custom headers use the existing mapping control. Sample files use the selected unit. Error-free all-or-nothing validation remains required. Every import row has a source row ID (see `importRecords` below and saved batches): a row whose source and row ID were imported before is skipped when its data is the same and is a row error when it differs. Payment evidence keeps its own rules as well: the same source event (source and eventId) or, without event IDs, the same source, reference, payer, amounts, currency, connection and batch under a new row ID is a row error, as is the same source event with different details; a row that only shares a reference is imported and reconciliation merges it or holds it as a conflict.

Imports expose source columns, a bounded parsed row preview and the skipped count alongside validation/import totals. Mapping remains explicit, and a changed file, mapping or kind requires a fresh preview. A check (an import preview, a saved batch's check and a commit's) also gives `warnings` when a record's name or reference comes from a fallback while a column is left unused: the name from the reference (or the row number), a generated reference. A column is unused when it is skipped or names no field of the kind (`importFieldsOf` in the shared schema), because the importer keeps such a column only as extra detail that nothing reads; the row identity column is not counted unless its header looks like the field that fell back, since the row ID column fills a field only when the mapping maps it there or its header is `reference` or `eventId`. Each warning is one sentence for the field, lists the unused columns and says when one looks like the field; a fallback with every column in use is taken as the source's own choice and does not warn. A warning never refuses the check or the commit. Before this, a customer file whose names were in `full_name` passed as valid and saved each customer named after its reference. The console suggests a field for a recognisable header that is no field itself (`suggestImportField`: `full_name` as the name, `due_date` as the due date, a header written as a field's shared label, such as Consent source or reference, a customer reference as the customer's own reference in a customer file and as the link to the customer elsewhere). The quick import reads the file's header in the browser (`csvHeader`, which skips blank lines before it as the importer does), so its columns are matched, and a row ID column headed Source row ID, `source_row_id` or `row_id` chosen (`suggestRowIdColumn`), before the first check; a row ID column whose header names the reference or event ID stays mapped to that field unless the operator chooses Row ID only; Import batches shows the suggestion in the mapping after a check as an unsaved change to save and check. Both sample files are headed with the shared labels. Before committing a batch, or importing sample data, whose check warns, the console asks for confirmation, naming the fallbacks. Downloaded errors escape spreadsheet formula prefixes. Files remain synthetic-only, up to 500 rows and 1.5 MB, with all-or-nothing commits.
`importRecords`: kind customers/mandates/due-items/attempts/observations, csv text, `identityColumn`, syntheticOnly=true, commit=false for preview; commit=true to persist. Header contract matches record properties plus data fields above. Default download a sample CSV for chosen kind. No real data!

The row ID is kept with the record, so it is screened under its column's header as a saved batch screens every cell: a raw account number in a column such as `account_number` is refused with 400 ("Do not enter a full bank account number. Enter a masked number, for example •••• 1234."), whether or not the request has an Idempotency-Key. The column is the row's identity and fills no field unless the mapping maps it to one or its header is `reference` or `eventId`. Rows are recognised across imports by the lender's one quick-import source, `Quick import`, and the row ID, stored in the record's `data.importIdentity` (`source`, `rowId`, `fingerprint`, with no `batchId`): importing the same file again creates nothing new (each row is `duplicate`, and `skipped` counts them), a row ID imported before with different data is a row error ("This source row ID was already imported with different data. …"), and a new row ID whose reference another saved record of the kind already has is a row error rather than a skipped row, as in a saved batch. Clients that relied on a reference-free file importing again, or on a row with an existing reference being skipped, must send stable row IDs. Records a quick import saved before this change carry no import identity, so they are not recognised by row ID: importing such a file again, a row whose reference one of them has is a row error, which holds back the whole import, and a row without a reference is imported again, so remove the rows already imported before importing such a file again. A `/v1/imports` commit journaled before this change has no `identityColumn`, so retrying it from Operations is refused with 400; import the file again with a row ID column instead. A quick-imported record carries this identity for recognition only: unlike a batch-imported one (`fromImportBatch`), it has no batch to correct it from, so the record API edits it as before.

Each row's `message` is written for the operator: every failing rule of the row, the import's own conflicts included (a row ID imported before with different data, a reference another saved record of the kind has, a source event saved with different details), one sentence for each column, naming the column as the shared label (`importFieldLabel` in the shared schema) and, where the operator's CSV header differs, that header ("Consent source or reference (column consent): Enter a value; it is blank on this row."), or "No column is mapped to Due date. Map the column that holds it." when no column fills a required field. Codes are listed with their labels ("Use Active (active) or Inactive (inactive)."), a link that names no record says which ("No customer has the reference or ID “C-9” in this lender."), and amount wording follows the source unit. A field no CSV cell can fill, such as a list Valo Pay sets, says "A CSV column cannot fill this field. Choose Skip column for it.", and no row error shows a raw field path or zod's own text. An invalid row's `detail` keeps every problem in the record API's words, field names included; the console's errors CSV has it as a Technical detail column. ## Bounded console read endpoints

- `GET /v1/queues/{queue}` accepts literal `q` search in addition to the existing view, owner, type, target, limit and offset. Totals and priority counts reflect the complete searched queue. A row matches when its name or reference, or its customer's name or reference, contains the term, ignoring case and accents. The term is matched once per queue row, and a customer's name once per customer, so a searched queue costs about what an unsearched one does.
- `GET /v1/reconciliation/{queue}` accepts proposals, duplicates, payments, observations, audit or batches; it returns items, related records, total, offset and asOf. The payments queue holds the money waiting for Finance: unallocated payments, and partial or overpaid payments that still hold unapplied money. An optional dueItem restricts linked review to that lender's instalment; an unavailable focus returns an empty result. Audit adds complete sample metadata.
- `GET /v1/close-history` returns compact items, total, allTotal and first/latest summaries across the complete inclusive WAT date range. Invalid or reversed dates return 400.
- `GET /v1/close-history/{id}` fetches one lender-scoped close with its full evidence; another lender's close returns 404.
- `GET /v1/reports?includeCloses=false` omits the historical close array while retaining all operational, billing and experiment measures. Omitting the option keeps the close array, in which closes more than a week before the latest carry their summary, as every lender load has them (the measures use only what a summary keeps); `GET /v1/close-history/{id}` returns any close whole.

All paged reads clamp deleted last pages, cap each page at 100 and remain inside the scoped repository. These read models never create writable partial snapshots. Desktop Chromium and Firefox and phone Chromium and WebKit tests use `artifacts/valo-pay/e2e`; database parity and isolation tests use `artifacts/api-server/tests/console-read-models.integration.test.ts`.

## Provider ingress

The console never calls `POST /v1/providers/paystack/{connectionId}/events`: it is the webhook address an operator registers with a Paystack test account (`docs/paystack.md`). It is off unless the host sets `VALOPAY_PAYSTACK_INGRESS=test`, and it checks each delivery's signature on its raw bytes before it locks or reads a lender, so a forged or tampered delivery gets 401 and nothing else. What it saves appears on `/sources`, in the Paystack test connection panel, through `GET /v1/sources` (`paystack.events`), as test-mode evidence only. There Finance and administrators may recheck a saved receipt (`POST /v1/sources/events/{id}/replay`) only while it is neither quarantined, a rejected fixture nor verified, the receipts the service refuses to replay (409). An event's `replayCount` counts those rechecks, not the operator's verification checks (`docs/paystack-test-verification.md`); earlier builds counted both.

## Search and history paging

The priority queues distinguish an unmatched search from an empty lender queue. Clearing the search keeps the status, owner and type filters. Reconciliation exposes the same explicit search form across its views and applies customer/payment/instalment reference matching before server counts and pages. Its monthly precision sample measures remain unfiltered.

The customer page reads `/api/v1/customers/{id}/history`. Events, mandates, instalments and payments have independent bounded pages, full counts and corrected offsets. The position is calculated from every related record in the same read transaction. It (`CustomerPosition`, as the timeline returns it too) gives obligationsKobo, allocatedKobo, outstandingKobo and unallocatedKobo, the customer's unapplied credit in naira only, and, only when the customer's payments in another currency hold unapplied money, `unallocatedOtherCurrencies`: that money by currency code, `{ "USD": { "count": 1, "amount": 100000 } }`, each with how many payments hold it and their amount in that currency's minor unit, as the dispute pack lists it; it is never added to the naira. Selected-record links may fetch one additional record, scoped to that customer and lender. Exports retain their full-history contract. The older timeline endpoint remains available to existing clients. No partial history response is a writable domain snapshot.

## Reviewed contract dates and experience refinements · 29 September 2026

Commercial records retain the existing v1 shape and gain optional `discountStartDate`, `fullPriceStartDate`, `discountTermsReference` and service-owned `discountReview`. PATCH merges omitted fields before validation, checks the current version and preserves an unchanged proposal and its confirmation. Changing signed flags, dates or reference proposes them afresh for a second person to confirm (`confirm_discount_terms`), or withdraws a proposal the flags no longer allow. Partial, invalid, mid-month or inverted dates are refused; this release does not define daily proration. Existing incomplete agreements can remain stored, but cannot issue new invoices until their dates are proposed and confirmed. Malformed legacy review metadata is treated as unavailable evidence rather than crashing Reports. The approved rule is prospective: issued invoice bytes and linked correction rates are preserved. From the review fixes of 29 September, one person's saved dates are a proposal only: a review stamped before them counts as a proposal awaiting confirmation, and new invoices carry the confirmation in `terms.discountReview`.

The billing answer adds optional `pricingReady`, `pricingExplanation`, `nextInvoicePricingReady` and `nextInvoicePricingExplanation`; an explanation names the actual cause in the words of the invoice refusal. A blocked current period has no priced statement lines and `totalKobo: null`; the independently computed next-period flag controls invoice availability. `billing.unitEconomics` adds `pricingReady`; unavailable licence, usage, recurring and annualised revenue are null, and its note names the cause. Costs and underlying receipt facts remain available. The answer also adds optional `rateDiscrepancies` and `rateDiscrepancyGuidance` (see `getReports`); they never block issuing a new invoice. The console shows the cause and unavailable values without converting them to zero, and lists the rate differences, each with its explanation, with the guidance; old answers without these metadata fields retain their existing presentation. Existing valid full-price statements and issued invoice totals are unchanged.

Cash Desk stores its selected section in the URL while retaining other query values and safe in-memory drafts. Shared section navigation uses ordinary accessible buttons and named content regions. Reports, Cash and Connections disclose supporting evidence progressively; print expansion restores the prior disclosure state afterwards. Payment progress derives only from saved server states. It shows an authorised or pending checkout alike, as awaiting a verified receipt, and gives distinct guidance for a checkout awaiting authorisation, an unknown outcome, a failure, a cancellation, a recorded receipt, a refund and a reversal. It shows no settlement: a recorded receipt is not presented as settled. Missing credit liquidity renders as unavailable while a genuine zero remains zero. These presentation changes do not confer authority, dispatch effects or record optimistic financial success.
